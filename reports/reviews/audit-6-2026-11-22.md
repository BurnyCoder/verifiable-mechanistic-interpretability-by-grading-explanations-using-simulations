# Sixth independent audit — 2026-11-22

*Made without reading the earlier reviews or audit scripts, after every round-2 number had been seen. No new agent
runs, no API spend. Results are at N = 3 — not yet N = 10. Preregistration deviation 13 records the changes.*

## 1. Scope and method

The audit had four parts.

1. **Hypothesis verdicts.** Every round-2 verdict was re-derived from the raw run records with code written for this
   audit (`scripts/audit6/verdicts.py`). It imports no project analysis code. It uses exact methods: the
   Jonckheere–Terpstra null by enumerating every distinct assignment of runs to levels, scipy's exact Mann–Whitney and
   Wilcoxon tests, and the full bootstrap distribution for three-run intervals.
2. **Scores.** The scores those tests use were re-graded by a third implementation (`scripts/audit6/regrade_numpy.py`).
   It is numpy only and was written from Nanda et al. (2023, §3), `ARCHITECTURE.md` and preregistration §4. It reads
   the targets straight from the published safetensors checkpoints.
3. **Methods.** The metric, statistics and Clock/Pizza code was read against its sources: `grader/metrics.py`,
   `grader/score.py`, `analysis/stats.py`, `analysis/hypotheses.py`, `analysis/clock_pizza.py`, `sensitivity.py`,
   `explainer/ladder.py`, `nonagent.py`, and grokviz's restricted loss and key-frequency rule.
4. **Claims and citations.** Three parallel source checks opened every cited paper, post, repository and doc: method
   sources, automated-interpretability and 2026 sources with product facts, and the two literature reviews. Every
   discrepancy was then checked again by hand against the source text. The paper's prose was also scanned for numbers
   typed by hand outside the generated markers.

### 2.3 Methods against their sources

- **KL.** KL(P_M ‖ P_M̂), in nats, over the 113 number logits. This is the direction ACDC uses: D_KL(G ‖ H), with the
  full model first. Correct.
- **HS.** Ridge on centred training folds with λ = 1e-6·tr(XᵀX)/d. R² is pooled about the training-fold mean and
  clipped. Harmonic mean per site, mean over three sites. This matches §4 and Amendment 1.
- **Invariances.** Regression R² is invariant only in its predictor (Kornblith et al. Table 1, "Y only", where Y is the
  predictor). The repo states this correctly.
- **Secondary metrics.** Linear CKA follows Kornblith's formula. Procrustes is Ding et al.'s squared distance (eq. 7).
  Matched-neuron correlation is a signed one-to-one assignment (Li et al.'s bipartite matching). The orthogonal-with-scale
  variant is Schönemann's solution. The whitened soundness is a held-out analogue of mean squared canonical correlation.
- **Statistics.** JT counts ties as ½. Holm is step-down. "Smallest attainable p" is 1/C(n₁+n₂, n₁) for Mann–Whitney
  and 1/2ⁿ for Wilcoxon. The 75 %/80 % coverage of three-run percentile intervals follows from the interval being the
  range of the runs. Hesterberg (2015) supports the general claim ("badly under-covers in small samples").
- **Clock/Pizza diagnostics.** Distance irrelevance equals Zhong et al.'s Def. 4.2: the mean over d of the std of the
  correct logit at fixed a − b, divided by the overall std. Gradient symmetricity is Def. 4.1 over 100 random triples
  (their footnote 3). The §4.1.2 ranges (Pizza 0–0.4, Clock 0.4–1) and §4.1.3's "decisive" are quoted correctly.
- **Restricted loss.** H8 uses grokviz's restricted loss: logits projected onto the key-frequency cos/sin(w_k(a+b))
  directions, bias-corrected, over all pairs. This is the paper code's `calculate_trig_loss`, and it reproduces ARENA's
  5.5474e-8 on the paper model. Nanda et al.'s text does not say which inputs the restricted loss uses. The code does.
- **Preregistration timing.** Git confirms the order:
  - preregistration first committed `08dcc51`, 2026-11-21 01:32;
  - protocol freeze `801edf0` (tag `protocol-freeze`), 12:19;
  - first reported runs and T-new trained, `fa2935d`, 12:33;
  - first hypotheses file, 15:26.

## 5. What a reviewer should still weigh

- **N = 3 and one simulator.** H2 cannot pass at this N under either reading, so the claim "HS separates true from
  wrong explanations" rests on H1's trend (driven by the E1 → E2 step), H3 (a comparison of means), H4 and H7. All of
  these contrast the right key frequencies with wrong or absent ones.
- **What HS adds on this task.** Post hoc, the preregistered secondary embedding-spectrum cosine orders the T0 builds
  almost as HS does: E2–E4 0.89–0.92, W-alg and W-scramble 0.92–0.94, W-freq 0.02, E1 0.05. It is a statistic of
  W_E's per-frequency norms and needs no forward pass. HS adds discrimination mainly for W-role (spectrum cosine 0.53,
  HS 0.20), where frequencies are misplaced across components. Together with the Clock/Pizza and scrambled-table
  non-separations, this is consistent with the paper's narrowed claim that HS is valid for frequency-level content.
  The paper's §5 now reports it.
- **The agent's repair.** 44 of 96 runs announce searches over constants, and every run checks task accuracy. A score
  therefore measures the explanation together with label-guided repair. Only the agent-free compiler measures the
  explanation without it.
- **Post-hoc analyses.** These are the Clock/Pizza diagnostics, the H8 phase split, memorisation and the spectrum
  comparison. They are labelled as post hoc. None feeds a verdict.
