## Bottom line

The statistics are right and the preregistered verdicts follow §5 as written. Every stored test statistic I
re-derived with my own exact enumeration reproduces. The grader implements §4, and the post-hoc Clock/Pizza diagnostic
implements Zhong et al.'s Def. 4.2. Nothing found invalidates a preregistered verdict.

The substantive problems are interpretive, and they all narrow the paper's central story:

1. The "logit-distilled" students copied T0's output **probabilities**, not its logits.
2. On this task an output-only comparison at the **logit** level separates the explanations about as well as HS. So
   the dissociation the paper reports is between output probabilities and mechanism, not between outputs in general
   and mechanism.
3. The paper said that no Clock-side build of an explanation exists. Five do, and they score no higher HS than the
   Pizza-side builds.

A pre-specified post-hoc baseline (logit-matched students) tests point 2 directly (§4 below). Only one of three met its
training criterion; that one gives a "partial" reading (HS 0.849, just under the 0.85 bound), so the hidden states carry
some information beyond the logits, by the evidence of one network.

## 2. Findings and fixes

**F1. The distilled students copy probabilities, not logits (major).** `training.train_from` (mode "distill")
minimises KL(T0 ‖ student) at temperature 1. T0's softmax puts at least 0.9999998 on the answer, so wrong-class
logits get almost no gradient; Hinton et al. (2015, §2) raise the temperature for exactly this reason, and show that
matching zero-mean logits is the high-temperature limit (§2.1, https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.02531).

- The students match T0's probabilities (KL 1.5–3.0e-6).
- They do not match its logits: centred-logit FVU 0.94–1.37 against T0, worse than predicting each input's mean logit.
  The E3 replacements reach 0.12–0.21.

Some descriptions were therefore wrong at the logit level: "distilled on M's full logits" (paper §3.5, baselines table,
model card), "full behavioural access" (round-1 report, plan), and "retrains and students reproduce T0's outputs more
closely than the explanation-built replacements" (paper §4.2). This is not a deviation, because §7 specified no
temperature. *Fix:* every description now says "trained to match M's output probabilities (KL at temperature 1)".

**F2. A logit-level output comparison separates the explanations like HS (major, post hoc).** T0's centred logits
follow Σ_k α_k cos(w_k(a + b − c)) (Nanda et al. §4.2), so they carry the key frequencies. The preregistered secondary
centred-logit FVU already separates the conditions on T0:

- W-freq, W-role, E0 and E1 score ≥ 1.03; E3, E4, W-alg and W-scramble score ≤ 0.27.
- E2 scatters (0.38–2.98), but only because E2 states no logit scale.

With one fitted positive scale per network, which mirrors the preregistered temperature-calibrated KL
(1 − cos² of centred logits, `logit_fit.calibrated_logit_fvu`), the comparison orders the explanations as HS does:

| Networks | Scale-calibrated centred-logit FVU |
|---|---|
| E2 | 0.36–0.43 |
| E3 | 0.12–0.21 |
| E4 | 0.08–0.14 |
| W-alg | 0.23 |
| W-scramble | 0.07–0.10 |
| W-freq, W-role, E0, E1 | 0.97–1.00 |
| Retrains, students, random networks | 0.87–1.00 |

So on this task output KL fails because it compares near-one-hot probabilities, not because outputs carry no
mechanism information. *Fix:* §4.2 now carries a generated table and paragraph, and the abstract, contributions (iii),
§5 "Why output KL fails", Limitations and Conclusion are narrowed. "Behaviour is not mechanism" becomes "output
probabilities are not mechanism". No verdict changes: H3 defines behaviour by argmax agreement and KL.

**F3. Clock-side builds exist (major, factual).** The paper, the README, a code comment and deviation 12(c) said that no
Clock-side build of an explanation exists, so how a Clock-like replacement would score "is not measured". By the
project's own reading rule (q ≥ 0.4, `results/controls/clock_pizza.json`), five agent builds are Clock-side: E4 s0 and
s1 (q 0.463, 0.464) and W-scramble s0–s2 (0.505, 0.658, 0.536).

Among the builds that carry T0's frequencies:

- the Clock-side builds score HS 0.91–0.95;
- the Pizza-side builds (E2, E3, E4 s2, W-alg) score 0.89–0.97.

Being on T0's side of the divide does not raise HS. This is descriptive, and side and explanation are confounded.
*Fix:* `tables.clock_side_split` generates the comparison; the key results, §5, the README and the deviation are
corrected; E4 is no longer called Pizza-like (its mean of 0.41 hides a 2–1 split).

**F4. E0 above E1 has a measurable cause (moderate).** Two of E0's three runs built eight consecutive frequencies.
They score HS 0.191 and 0.194, and one of them shares almost nothing with T0 (share of W_L on T0's frequencies 0.01).
Every E1 build concentrates on the paper's four or five frequencies (0.045–0.048). Broad-spectrum networks sit near
the random-initialisation level (0.24). So "E0 > E1" reflects HS's floor, not information in E0's text. *Fix:* a
generated sentence in §4.1, plus a pointer in the abstract.

**F5. "No significant difference" for the students comparison (moderate, statistical wording).** All six E3/E4 runs
exceed all three students, but a 3-vs-3 two-sided exact test cannot go below p = 0.10 (Holm 0.20). A non-significant
result from a test that cannot reach α is not evidence of equality (Altman & Bland 1995, BMJ 311:485,
https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.311.7003.485). *Fix:* the line now reads "not significant (Holm p 0.20; smallest
attainable p 0.10; every agent run above every student; post-hoc rule: cannot tell at this N)".

**F6. The multiplicity line was method-dependent (minor).** It compared Holm-adjusted H4 p-values (0.009) with
0.05/8 and concluded that H4 "would not survive". Holm over all nine confirmatory p-values (each test's own p) keeps
H1's HS trend (8.5e-4) and both H4 tests (0.036), and drops H7 (0.094). *Fix:* both readings are generated.

## 3. Methods: used as their fields use them?

- **Rank tests and multiplicity:** Jonckheere–Terpstra for ordered alternatives, Mann–Whitney with Holm, Wilcoxon
  signed-rank on paired margins, and exact nulls throughout are standard and correctly applied. Rank-biserial r is
  Kerby's 2U/(n₁n₂) − 1.
- **Bootstrap intervals:** the percentile intervals at n = 3 are, as the paper says, the range of the runs (coverage
  ≈ 75 % for a symmetric distribution).
- **HS as regression R²:** used with the invariances Kornblith et al. (Table 1) give. The soundness direction's basis
  dependence and the wider-than-symmetry alignment at `mlp_post` are disclosed, with a whitened check.
- **Output KL:** the direction is Conmy et al.'s. Its failure here is a property of comparing near-one-hot
  probabilities (F2), which the paper now says.
- **Distillation:** temperature-1 KL is a standard choice, but it does not transfer logit structure from a peaked
  teacher (Hinton et al. §2). That is why F1 is a wording error, not a methods error, and why a logit-matched baseline
  is the right test.
- **Distance irrelevance:** Zhong et al.'s definition on raw correct logits; robust to centring here.

## 4. Pre-specified post-hoc baseline: logit-matched students

Design, training criterion and reading were committed and pushed before training (deviation 16(a), commit 649ffa3):

- three students from the existing students' initialisations;
- the same recipe and 20,000 steps;
- squared error between centred logits;
- trained if centred-logit FVU < 0.05;
- reading: HS ≥ 0.85 means HS adds little beyond the logits; HS ≤ 0.35 with the criterion met means the hidden states
  carry information that matched logits do not.

Result (`results/baselines/T0.json`, `students_logit`; each student took about 45 minutes on the GPU):

| Student (init seed) | centred-logit FVU vs T0 | criterion met? | HS | KL_out | task accuracy |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1000 | 0.165 | no | 0.783 | 0.232 | 0.914 |
| 1001 | 0.051 | no (narrowly; with one fitted scale 0.048, but the criterion is the raw value) | 0.778 | 0.013 | 0.997 |
| 1002 | 0.023 | yes | 0.849 | 0.0009 | 0.9998 |

By the pre-specified rule only student 1002 is interpreted. Its HS, 0.849, is just under the 0.85 bound, so the
pre-specified reading is **partial**.

- Descriptively, it matches T0's logits more closely than any agent or compiler build (FVU 0.023; E3 agent builds
  0.12–0.21), yet scores lower HS than every E2–E4 build (0.889–0.965).
- A closer logit match therefore does not imply a higher HS, which suggests that the hidden states carry some
  mechanism information beyond the logits. This rests on one network, post hoc.
- The two students that missed the criterion score 0.78 and are not interpreted.
- The 20,000-step recipe with weight decay 1 was not enough for reliable logit matching. A recipe that meets the
  criterion is a next step (`reports/next-steps.md`, J1).
